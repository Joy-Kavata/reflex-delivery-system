# Reflex — Problem & Solution

## 1. The Problem

Small electronics and accessories retailers in Nairobi often coordinate local deliveries using WhatsApp messages and phone calls.

This approach can work when delivery volumes are low, but becomes difficult to manage as the number of orders increases.

The main problem is not that retailers lack a way to communicate.

The problem is that delivery information is **fragmented across conversations and calls instead of being managed through one shared workflow.**

---

## 2. What Goes Wrong

A typical delivery may involve several separate conversations:

1. A customer places an order.
2. Retailer staff records the customer's delivery details.
3. The retailer contacts a rider through WhatsApp or phone.
4. The rider receives the delivery information.
5. The rider picks up the item.
6. The retailer contacts the rider to ask for an update.
7. The rider confirms delivery through a message or call.

This creates several visibility problems.

### Assignment Visibility

Staff may need to search through conversations to determine which rider was given an order.

### Status Visibility

The retailer may not know whether an order is still waiting, has been picked up or has already been delivered without contacting the rider.

### Delivery History

Important delivery information may remain inside individual WhatsApp conversations or phone-call records rather than a structured delivery history.

### Confirmation

Proof or confirmation of delivery may depend on a message or verbal confirmation, making it harder to review deliveries later.

---

## 3. The Core User Need

The users do not necessarily need another messaging platform.

They need a **shared source of truth for delivery coordination.**

The key question Reflex aims to answer is:

> **"What is happening with this delivery right now?"**

A retailer should be able to check the system instead of repeatedly asking staff or riders for updates.

---

## 4. The Solution

Reflex converts the informal delivery process into a structured workflow.

Instead of:

**WhatsApp → Phone Call → WhatsApp → Phone Call**

the process becomes:

**Request → Assign → Pick Up → Deliver → Confirm**

Each delivery has a record and a current status that can be viewed by the appropriate users.

---

## 5. How Reflex Works

### Step 1 — Retailer Creates a Request

The retailer enters:

* Customer name
* Customer phone number
* Delivery address
* Item description

The delivery becomes a **Requested** delivery.

### Step 2 — Dispatcher Assigns a Rider

The dispatcher sees open delivery requests and assigns an available rider.

The delivery changes to:

**Assigned**

### Step 3 — Rider Picks Up the Order

The rider views their assigned delivery and updates its status after collecting the item.

The delivery changes to:

**Picked Up**

### Step 4 — Rider Completes the Delivery

After delivering the order, the rider updates the delivery.

The delivery changes to:

**Delivered**

### Step 5 — Retailer Can See the Outcome

The retailer can check the delivery record and see its current or completed status without having to contact the rider for every update.

---

## 6. Why a Small Retailer Would Use Reflex

Reflex is not trying to replace every tool a retailer uses.

It focuses on one operational problem: **delivery coordination and visibility.**

A small retailer would use Reflex because it provides:

* One place to view delivery requests
* Clear rider assignment
* Visible delivery status
* A delivery history
* Less dependence on individual WhatsApp conversations and phone calls

The value is therefore not simply "having an app."

The value is:

> **Turning delivery coordination from a conversation-based process into a trackable workflow.**

---

## 7. Why Start With Electronics Retailers?

The MVP focuses on small electronics and accessories retailers because their deliveries are often relatively small, frequent and locally handled by riders.

Typical products include:

* Phone chargers
* Cables
* Earphones and headphones
* Power banks
* Phone cases

This gives the team a focused customer segment instead of trying to design for every type of retailer at once.

The same coordination model could later be adapted for other small businesses such as pharmacies or hardware stores.

---

## 8. MVP Value Proposition

> **Reflex gives small retailers a simple shared workflow for creating, assigning and tracking deliveries, reducing the uncertainty created by scattered WhatsApp messages and phone calls.**

The MVP succeeds if a retailer can answer three questions without making a call:

1. **What deliveries are open?**
2. **Who is handling this delivery?**
3. **What is its current status?**

---

## 9. What Reflex Does Not Try to Solve Yet

The MVP deliberately does not attempt to solve:

* Live GPS tracking
* Route optimization
* Automatic rider assignment
* Integrated payments
* Advanced analytics
* Offline operation

These are future opportunities rather than MVP requirements.

The product first validates the core coordination problem before introducing additional complexity.

---

## 10. Product Principle

Reflex follows the **KISS — Keep It Simple** principle.

The MVP focuses on one clear transformation:

**From fragmented delivery communication → to structured delivery coordination.**
